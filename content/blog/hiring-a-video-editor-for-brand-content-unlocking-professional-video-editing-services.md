Video is now the format audiences expect from brands, on every platform. Yet raw footage rarely tells a story on its own. Editing is where pacing, sound, colour and message come together, and it is often the difference between a video people scroll past and one they remember.

This guide explains what a professional editor brings to your brand, when it makes sense to hire one, and how to brief them well.

## What a professional video editor actually does

A good editor does far more than trim clips. Their work shapes how your brand is perceived.

- **Story structure.** They select the strongest moments and arrange them so the video has a clear beginning, middle and end.
- **Pacing for the platform.** A 15-second reel and a two-minute brand film need very different rhythms. Editors cut for the way people watch on each channel.
- **Colour and sound.** Consistent colour grading, clean audio and music that suits the mood make content feel premium.
- **Brand consistency.** Logos, fonts, captions and end cards are applied the same way every time, so viewers recognise your content instantly.
- **Versions for every channel.** One shoot can become vertical reels, square feed posts and widescreen videos for YouTube or your website.

## Signs it is time to hire an editor

You may be ready for professional editing if:

- You have plenty of footage but no time to turn it into finished content.
- Your videos look different every time and do not feel like one brand.
- Viewers drop off in the first few seconds.
- You publish on several platforms and need multiple formats from each shoot.
- Video is becoming central to your marketing, sales or recruitment.

## What to look for when hiring

### A portfolio that matches your style

Look for work similar to what you need, whether that is fast-paced social content, polished corporate films or product videos.

### Knowledge of each platform

The best editors understand hooks, captions, aspect ratios and attention spans on Instagram, YouTube and LinkedIn.

### A clear process and turnaround

Ask how feedback rounds work, how long a first cut takes and what the final deliverables include.

### Brand thinking, not only software skills

An editor who asks about your audience and goals will produce work that supports your marketing, not just work that looks good.

## How to brief a video editor

A strong brief saves time and revisions. Include:

1. **The goal.** Awareness, product launch, recruitment or sales.
2. **The audience.** Who should watch, and what they care about.
3. **The platform.** Where the video will be published, and in which formats.
4. **References.** Two or three videos whose style you like.
5. **Brand assets.** Logo files, fonts, colours and any music guidelines.
6. **The deadline.** When you need the first cut and the final files.

## In-house, freelance or agency?

An in-house editor suits brands producing video every day. Freelancers can work well for occasional projects. An agency gives you editing alongside strategy, shooting and distribution, which helps when video is part of a wider marketing plan and you want one team accountable for the result.
